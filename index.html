§5

Ionisation energy (Koopmans' theorem)

Koopmans' theorem is strictly a Hartree–Fock result: −εHOMO approximates the ionisation energy — the energy to remove the outermost electron of whatever species is currently selected — because removing an electron leaves the remaining orbitals unrelaxed. It is not formally justified for Hartree or the DFT functionals, but the same −εHOMO estimate is shown for every configuration below to make the effect of self-interaction error on the orbital energy itself visible — a negative value means that method predicts the outermost electron is not bound at all. The experimental reference row only appears for a neutral atom (§1's charge set to 0): for an ion it would need a different, further ionisation energy (2nd, 3rd, …) that isn't tabulated here.

§6

Self-interaction, orbital by orbital

For the outermost (HOMO) orbital, this is its Hartree self-Coulomb energy Jself — the spurious repulsion an electron would feel from its own charge cloud — alongside how much of it each method's exchange treatment cancels. For Hartree–Fock this is an exact identity (the same-spin exchange integral of an orbital with itself equals its own direct integral, so the cancellation is exactly Jself). For the DFT functionals (LDA, PBE, rSCAN) it is only an approximate, orbital-projected diagnostic, since their potential depends on the total density rather than a single orbital — the numbers can occasionally show slight over- or under-cancellation (even a small negative residual), unlike Hartree–Fock's exact identity.

§7

Orbital shape in 3D

A three-dimensional view of Rnl(r) combined with a real spherical harmonic, rendered as a cloud of points sampled in proportion to the actual probability density |ψ|² — the same way the radial plots are built, just in 3D instead of integrated over angle. Colour marks the sign of ψ (important for seeing where an orbital changes phase — at an angular node for p/d/f shapes, or at a radial node for any excited shell). The threshold slider is a density cutoff: raise it and only the densest, most probable core of the cloud remains, giving an isosurface-like shrinking boundary without needing to extract an explicit surface mesh. "Total electron density" (first entry in the orbital dropdown) sums every occupied shell instead of showing one at a time — perfectly spherically symmetric by construction, so no phase to colour and no shape to choose, but a direct view of the atom's overall size and concentric shell structure. §8

Basis set convergence

What "basis set quality" actually buys you, for the current element and charge (§1): total energy under minimal (Slater's historical rule), double-zeta+ (double-zeta plus one spare diffuse function) with its fixed exponent spread, the same with that spread variationally optimised for this atom (done as part of this section's own sweep), and even-tempered — climbing, to greater or lesser degree, toward the near-exact numerical result shown as a reference line. Worth watching for: the optimised double-zeta basis, with half as many functions as even-tempered, can still come out ahead of it — exponent quality matters as much as basis size, sometimes more. Hartree-Fock is the clean case, since it minimises the quantity plotted here and the bars should improve steadily toward the reference; for Hartree and the DFT functionals, don't be surprised if a larger basis sometimes reports a worse (less negative) bar than a smaller one — those methods don't minimise the quantity plotted here (see the Notes), so enlarging the basis only reliably helps the quantity each method genuinely does minimise, not necessarily this one. This runs several full self-consistent fields from scratch (one of them, the optimised double-zeta case, is itself a repeated-SCF search), so it's opt-in rather than automatic — the time depends strongly on the element and method (the status line gives an estimate; a numerical Hartree-Fock reference for a heavy atom can take many minutes).
